Critical Analysis & Literary Commentary


1. Theme of Jealousy and Manipulation

Iago's psychological manipulation of Othello represents one of literature's most devastating portrayals of green-eyed jealousy. Iago exploits Othello's insecurities regarding his race, age, and foreign status in Venice, turning Othello's noble trust into blind suspicion.

2. Conflict of Character: Othello vs. Iago

Othello embodies straightforward honor, battlefield valor, and emotional innocence. Conversely, Iago represents Machiavellian cunning, cynicism, and absolute cold malice without genuine remorse ("motiveless malignity").

3. The Symbolism of the Handkerchief

The strawberry-embroidered handkerchief acts as a central dramatic motif. To Othello, it symbolizes marital loyalty, magical heirloom heritage, and Desdemona's purity. In Iago's hands, it becomes fabricated physical evidence of infidelity.

4. Desdemona’s Tragic Innocence

Desdemona represents unconditional devotion and purity. Her defiance of traditional societal bounds to marry Othello highlights her courage, yet her pure simplicity makes her unable to comprehend Othello's irrational jealousy until it is too late.

5. Dramatic Irony & Tragedy

The audience watches with agonizing foresight as Iago weaves his web. The climax underlines tragic misjudgment: Othello loves "not wisely, but too well," ending in catastrophic loss before truth is uncovered.
