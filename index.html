🎭 Othello Chapter 2 · Interactive

Long time ago in Venice there was a rich Senator named Brabantio. He had a beautiful daughter, Desdemona. She was sought by many suitors, but she chose a Moor—Othello, a valiant general. She loved his stories of adventure, battles, and strange lands. They married privately. Brabantio accused Othello of witchcraft, but Othello's honest tale won the Duke's favor. Desdemona confirmed her love. They went to Cyprus to fight the Turks, but a tempest scattered the enemy fleet.

Othello trusted Cassio and promoted him to lieutenant, angering Iago. Iago plotted revenge, making Cassio drunk and causing a brawl. Othello demoted Cassio. Iago advised Cassio to seek Desdemona's help. She pleaded for Cassio, but Iago sowed jealousy in Othello's mind, hinting at an affair. Othello demanded proof. Iago used a handkerchief—Othello's first gift—planted on Cassio. Othello, convinced, smothered Desdemona. Cassio was wounded; Iago's treachery was exposed. Othello, in anguish, killed himself. Iago was executed.

🧠 Critical Analysis

Othello's tragic flaw is not jealousy alone, but his credulity and self-doubt as an outsider. Iago exploits racial insecurity. Desdemona embodies innocence and agency, yet her defence of Cassio becomes her undoing. Iago is machiavellian, motivated by spite. The handkerchief—a symbol of fidelity—becomes ocular proof. The play critiques racial prejudice, misogyny, and otherness.
