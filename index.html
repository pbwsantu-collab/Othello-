    Act-by-Act Breakdown & Literary Analysis

    Act I: Courtship & Accusation in Venice

    Brabantio accuses Othello of using spells and magic to win Desdemona. Othello proves to the Senate that his only witchcraft was the artless eloquence of his adventure stories.

    Act II: The Tempest & Dismissal of Cassio

    A violent storm disperses the Ottoman fleet. In Cyprus, Iago tricks Cassio into getting drunk and provoking a brawl, leading Othello to strip Cassio of his rank.

    Act III: Poisoning the Mind & The Handkerchief

    Iago manipulates Cassio's intercession request into proof of infidelity. He gets Emilia to steal the strawberry-patterned magical handkerchief and plants it in Cassio's lodging.

    Act IV: Jealousy & Accusation

    Driven to near madness by Iago's suggestions, Othello confronts Desdemona over the lost handkerchief and directly accuses her of unfaithfulness.

    Act V: Tragic Climax & Exposure

    Othello smothers Desdemona. Cassio and letters found on Roderigo reveal Iago's full villainy. Overwhelmed by remorse, Othello kills himself with his sword.

    Key Symbol: Strawberry Handkerchief

    Given by an Egyptian charmer to Othello's mother, it symbolizes marital fidelity. In Iago's plot, it becomes the crucial "ocular proof" driving the tragedy.
